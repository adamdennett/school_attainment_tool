# Getting it published in The Argus — pitch pack

**Recommendation: lead with the news pitch (Pathway 2), hold the 250–350 word letter (Pathway 1) as a fallback.** A reporter-written story carries more reach and credibility than a self-authored opinion piece, keeps Adam in the neutral-expert seat (above the admissions fray), and — if it lands — usually opens the door to running the full ~800-word attendance feature ([argus_attendance.md](argus_attendance.md)) as a guest piece. All figures below are verified against the analysis; sources noted for the fact-checker.

---

## OPTION A — News pitch email (send first)

**To:** news@theargus.co.uk
**Subject:** Story pitch — Brighton & Hove has England's second-worst school attendance: new UCL analysis shows the cost, and a way forward

Dear Newsdesk,

I'm a Brighton parent and Professor of Urban Analytics at UCL. Using the Government's own open data on every school in England, I've analysed what really drives secondary-school results in our city — and one finding stands out as both urgent and, I think, genuinely newsworthy for Argus readers: **Brighton and Hove had the second-worst school attendance in England last year, behind only Knowsley.**

That matters because attendance — not which school a child attends — turns out to be the single biggest lever on the results of our most disadvantaged children. My modelling suggests that if every school in the city matched the attendance of Brighton's own best-attended school, disadvantaged pupils could leave with **around five more GCSE grade points each** — a bigger gain than any other option on the table, and one that requires moving no child anywhere.

There's a positive, counterintuitive angle too. Once you account for the children they actually teach, Brighton and Hove's schools are **among the best in England for disadvantaged pupils** — including some, such as BACA in the north-east of the city, that are too often talked down. On outcomes we are near the top of the country; it is on attendance that we fall to the bottom.

I'd welcome the chance to talk it through with a reporter, or to write a short guest piece. My recent public talk (21 July) and the full analysis are freely available at [link], every figure is reproducible from public data, and I can supply high-resolution charts. I'm also making an open offer to work with the council, schools and community groups on a shared picture of attendance across the city — which may be of interest in itself as the new term and the autumn inspection round approach.

I can verify my identity and provide any further detail on request.

Best wishes,
Adam Dennett
[phone] · [email] · [postal address]

**Quote, ready to use:**
> "For a disadvantaged child, Brighton and Hove is actually one of the best places in England to go to school. The problem isn't our schools — it's that too many of our children aren't in them. Grasp attendance and this city could be genuinely the best in the country." — Professor Adam Dennett, UCL

**Key facts for your reporter (all from open DfE data; methods and code public):**
- Brighton & Hove = **2nd-highest absence of any local authority in England, 2024-25**, behind Knowsley. *Source: DfE local-authority absence statistics.*
- The school a child attends explains only about **10–15%** of the variation in their GCSE results; family, poverty and neighbourhood explain most — consistent with 60 years of research since the Coleman report.
- Reshuffling pupils between schools to even out disadvantage: worth about **1–2 Attainment 8 points**, and not for disadvantaged pupils.
- Matching the city's best-attended school on attendance: worth about **5 Attainment 8 points for disadvantaged pupils.**
- Brighton & Hove ranks about **7th of 152 English local authorities for disadvantaged pupils' value-added** (4th for all pupils).
- **BACA's disadvantaged value-added was +5 Attainment 8 points last year** — among the top ~10% of schools in England.

## OPTION B — Letter to the Editor / opinion (fallback, ~310 words)

**Submit via** theargus.co.uk/yourargus/sendaletter **or** letters@theargus.co.uk (subject: "Letter to the Editor"). Include full name, address and phone for verification.

**Headline suggestion:** *The real crisis in our schools is empty desks — and we can fix it*

Two years of argument about school admissions in Brighton and Hove missed the thing that matters most. Using the Government's open data on every school in England, I've looked at what really drives how well our children do — and the answer is not which school they attend. It is whether they attend at all.

Last year, Brighton and Hove had the second-worst school attendance in England. That is the emergency hiding in plain sight. A child who isn't in the room cannot be taught, and too many of ours aren't in the room.

Here is what makes it urgent — and hopeful. If every school in the city matched the attendance of our own best-attended school, disadvantaged children could leave with around five more GCSE grade points each. No child moved anywhere; no one loses. Nothing else on the table comes close.

And contrary to how we talk about our schools, they are good. Once you account for the children they actually teach, Brighton and Hove's schools are among the best in England for disadvantaged pupils — including some, like BACA, that get talked down unfairly.

Attendance is hard. Children miss school for reasons mostly not their fault: illness, anxiety, unmet special needs, the cost of the bus, families under strain. Campaigners like Class Divide have rightly shown how heavily these fall on our poorest families. Tackling it means taking those barriers seriously — not lecturing families or blaming schools.

So I'll make an offer. I'd like to work with the council, our schools and community groups to build one honest, shared picture of attendance across the city, and use it to get more children into school. We already have some of the best schools in the country for the children who need them most. Imagine what they could do if everyone turned up.

Adam Dennett, [address]

## Sequencing and practical notes

- **Send the news pitch first**, to news@theargus.co.uk. If you know the education or local-democracy reporter's name, address it to them and cc the newsdesk. Attach nothing heavy — offer charts, don't send them cold.
- **Wait ~5–7 days.** If no reply, submit **Option B** as a letter/opinion. (Don't run both simultaneously — a newsdesk that sees the letter already in the queue has less reason to commission the story.)
- **Timing is a live hook.** Send before the new term and the autumn inspection round — "why now" is doing work for you. Sooner is better while the 21 July talk is recent.
- **Imagery.** Offer one or two high-resolution, self-explanatory charts you hold copyright to (e.g. the city's attendance ranking, or the value-added "which schools are actually good" chart). Confirm you have permission for any school photos before offering them.
- **Verification.** The Argus needs your full name, address and phone to publish either format — have them ready; they won't print the address/phone.
- **Editorial control.** They can and will cut, condense and re-headline. Your headline suggestions are suggestions. The pitch is written so the *first paragraph* survives cutting intact.
- **Interview discipline (important for staying above the fray):** if a reporter tries to steer you back to the admissions row, decline to re-litigate it and redirect: "That debate has been had; what the data actually shows is that attendance is where the city's real gains are." Keep the story about attendance, the city's potential, and your offer — not about who was right about admissions.
- **Fact-check readiness.** Have the DfE LA absence release to hand for "second worst," and be ready to state plainly what the ~5-point and 1–2-point figures come from (your report's models on open data) if asked.

## Fact-check flags (confirm before sending)
- **"Second worst in England"** — our panel says 2nd (behind Knowsley); the citable public source is the **DfE local-authority absence statistics**, which are pupil/session-weighted and may rank marginally differently. Check the DfE release ranks B&H 2nd before printing that word; "one of the worst" is the safe fallback.
- **BACA +5 last year** — correct for 2024-25 (disadvantaged value-added, report's imputed model); the multi-year average is lower, so keep it to "last year."
- **7th / 4th value-added ranks** — from the report's caterpillar (disadvantaged 7th of 152, all-pupils 4th); confirm these match the current rendered report before quoting.
- **"~5 more GCSE grade points"** — best-attended-school attendance scenario (~5.1 all / 5.6 disadvantaged), report's own model and formula.
